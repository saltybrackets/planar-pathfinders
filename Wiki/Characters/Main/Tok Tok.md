## Appearance
- Wears a full-size white kabuki mask with red kanji-like symbols, beak-shaped so it covers his entire face. No facial expressions are ever visible — his emotion has to read entirely through head tilts, posture, gesture, and stillness/movement.

## Personality (Roleplay Notes)
- **Trope / Character Reference:** The wise old mentor — Mr. Miyagi's warmth and koan-like patience fused with Jiraiya's mischief, vanity, and appetite for a good bawdy joke. Kind first, funny second, and sneakily shrewd underneath both.
- **Voice:** Speaks plainly and a little accented, favoring short, poetic or proverb-shaped lines (the kabuki performer and poet in him can't help it). Undercuts his own wisdom with a wink or an off-color aside before it gets too heavy — never lets a lesson sit as pure solemnity. Peppers speech with small amused repeated exclamations — "Ha ha!", "Hoo hoo!", "Yes yes!" — as verbal tics of delight, agreement, or mischief.
- **Memorable Tidbit:** Ancient and extremely well-travelled — his history has Pinocchio-shaped echoes (something not-quite-human made whole through the journey), and somewhere in his past he was swallowed by an Astral Dreadnought while crossing the Astral Plane and escaped. He hasn't told this to anyone — no one at the table knows it's real. At the campaign's start he'll only ever gesture at it sideways, staged as a "fictional" kabuki performance piece: a fable about a small creature swallowed by a vast dark thing, told with full theatrical flourish, never framed as his own story.
- **Home:** Originally from Kara-Tur; has wandered long enough that no single plane really counts as home anymore.
- **Purpose:** Owns and runs the Wandering Tit, the tavern that's the party's home base — he is their employer and their mentor, the one who nudges them toward growth without ever lecturing.
- **Hobbies:** Wood carving, especially little dolls and marionettes — carved figures turn up around the tavern and occasionally as gifts. Surprisingly spry for how old and small he looks: his kabuki performances include real acrobatics and shadowboxing, not just stagecraft. Can throw his voice to mimic just about anyone or anything, a trick he uses constantly in performance.
- **Storytelling:** Often holds court at a table with patrons, spinning highly fantastic tales — lost loves, souls wandering for what seems like eternity across the stars, and a recurring favorite about a golem who wished to become a real boy and was swallowed by a huge whale (an echo of the true Dreadnought story, told as if it were just another yarn). Listeners get the sense some of these tales have a kernel of his own history buried in them, but he never confirms which.

**Roleplay cues:**
- Treats the party less like students and more like grandchildren he dotes on — advice comes from fondness and worry, not instruction. He's not testing them or grading their growth; he just wants good things for them and says so, often with a fuss.
- Poet and kabuki performer by trade/temperament: prone to a flourish, a dramatic pause, an unexpected verse, or a small staged "performance" out of an ordinary moment.
- Humor is warmer and raunchier than Miyagi's, closer to Jiraiya's — flirts with bawdiness, self-deprecation, and gentle teasing of the party, but it's never cruel.
- Eccentric, not aloof — his age and mileage show as odd tangents and non-sequiturs, not distance. He's fully present and fond of these people.
- Evasive about his own past whenever asked for specifics — he'll happily hint that he's been well-traveled and had plenty of adventures, but pointed questions get deflected with a joke, a tangent, or a story about someone else before he lets the topic go.
- The Dreadnought story, when it surfaces at all early on, must read as invented allegory/theater to any listener — no winking at the "real" audience, no tells that break the fiction. Only the DM (and this file) knows it's true.
